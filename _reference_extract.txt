P90|Normal|ABSTRACT
P92|Normal|The rapid proliferation of Underwater Wireless Sensor Networks (UWSNs) and terrestrial WSNs has revolutionized monitoring capabilities but remains hampered by limited battery life, high latency, and security vulnerabilities like black hole attacks. While various algorithms address these issues in isolation, a unified solution is lacking. This research introduces GEI-IGWO (Golden Eagle and Improved Grey Wolf Optimization), a novel hybrid routing protocol that synergizes the global exploration of the Golden Eagle Optimization Algorithm (GEOA) with the aggressive local exploitation of the Improved Grey Wolf Optimization (IGWO) to simultaneously optimize security, energy efficiency, and Quality of Service (QoS).
P94|Normal|The core innovation of GEI-IGWO is its strict adherence to a specialized multi-objective fitness function derived from five critical UWSN parameters: Global Node Trust to mitigate malicious behavior; Residual Energy to extend network lifetime; Distance to Sink to minimize transmission power; Intra-Cluster Average Distance to optimize node density; and Path Delay to ensure real-time QoS . By normalizing and weighting these conflicting objectives, the protocol ensures robust operation without compromising security for energy conservation.
P96|Normal|The protocol operates in two phases to maximize efficiency. Phase I utilizes GEOA for optimal Cluster Head (CH) selection, mathematically mapping attack vectors to select nodes with the highest trust and energy levels for load balancing . Phase II employs IGWO combined with a Protected Adaptive Mutation mechanism using a statistical t-distribution to escape local optima during the exploitation phase.
P98|Normal|Extensive MATLAB simulations benchmarking GEI-IGWO against standard protocols demonstrate its superiority. The hybrid model achieves a near-optimal coverage rate of 100% and significantly reduces total network energy consumption. Crucially, the integration of trust-based routing maintains a Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR) exceeding 95% even amidst security threats. These results confirm that GEI-IGWO provides a robust, scalable solution for resource-constrained networks, successfully navigating the trade-offs between energy conservation, data security, and latency.

INTRODUCTION
P172|Normal|1.1 Background of Underwater Wireless Sensor Networks (UWSN)
P173|Normal|With the rise of the Internet of Things (IoT), the application fields of sensor networks have continued to expand from agriculture to urban infrastructure monitoring. Among these, Underwater Wireless Sensor Networks (UWSNs) have emerged as a critical domain for oceanographic data collection, disaster early warning, and environmental monitoring. These networks are characterized by a collection of sensor nodes that possess limited energy, minimized speed, and low cost [3].
P174|Normal|However, the deployment of sensor nodes in such difficult-to-reach environments presents significant challenges. Unlike terrestrial networks, underwater environments impose harsh constraints, including long propagation delays, dynamic 3D mobility, and severe energy limitations. The sensor nodes are typically battery-powered, and the exhaustion of the battery during active data routing can result in the deterioration of the entire network's lifetime. Traditional designs often focus on improving either coverage or transmission efficiency in isolation, frequently ignoring the critical balance required for energy efficiency. Consequently, ensuring that data is transmitted reliably while maximizing the residual energy of nodes remains a primary operational hurdle [8].

P178|Normal|1.2 Motivation Behind the Study
P179|Normal|The motivation for this research stems from the limitations of existing routing protocols and the "No Free Lunch" theorem (NFLT), which suggests that no single optimization algorithm offers a universal solution for all problems. Traditional protocols like LEACH (Low Energy Adaptive Clustering Hierarchy) utilize random probability for Cluster Head (CH) selection, often failing to consider the residual energy of nodes, which leads to maximized energy consumption and premature network failure [6].
P180|Normal|While swarm-intelligence algorithms have shown potential in determining near-optimal solutions, most perform well in either exploration or exploitation but rarely both, resulting in the frequent selection of suboptimal CHs. Hybridization—combining the strengths of two distinct algorithms—is essential to balance global exploration and local exploitation to prevent unnecessary energy drain. For instance, recent studies on the Golden Eagle Optimization Algorithm (GEOA) hybridized with Improved Grasshopper Optimization (IGHOA) demonstrated significant improvements in preventing worst-node selection. Similarly, the Improved Grey Wolf Optimization (IGWO) has shown the ability to achieve 100% coverage of monitoring target points by effectively managing node energy consumption.
P181|Normal|Furthermore, motivation is drawn from the need to incorporate comprehensive "fitness parameters" that go beyond simple energy metrics. To enhance security and reliability, it is necessary to evaluate Global Node Trust to avoid malicious nodes. To ensure Quality of Service (QoS), Path Delay must be minimized for real-time monitoring. By integrating these specific parameters into a hybrid framework, this study aims to create a robust routing protocol that addresses the multifaceted challenges of UWSNs.
P183|Normal|1.3 Problem Statement
P184|Normal|Despite advancements in WSN protocols, the selection of inefficient Cluster Heads (CHs) remains a critical bottleneck. In hierarchical cluster-based networks, CHs consume maximal energy due to the additional overload of data aggregation and transmission to the base station. Existing solutions face the following specific problems:
P185|Normal|Inefficient CH Selection: Protocols that rely on random deployment or single-objective functions often result in "coverage blind spots" and the selection of nodes with low energy or poor connectivity as CHs.
P186|Normal|Imbalanced Optimization: Single-objective algorithms often lack the synergy to optimize energy efficiency and node coverage simultaneously, leading to inefficient energy utilization.
P187|Normal|Security and Latency Gaps: Many traditional methods fail to account for security threats (e.g., black hole attacks) or latency requirements, which are critical for maintaining network stability and timely data delivery.

P208|Normal|2.1 Underwater Wireless Sensor Networks (UWSN) Overview
P209|Normal|Underwater Wireless Sensor Networks (UWSNs) have garnered significant attention due to their wide range of applications, including oceanographic data collection, pollution monitoring, and disaster prevention. The architecture typically involves deploying sensor nodes underwater, which communicate acoustically with surface stations or sinks [11].
P210|Normal|However, UWSNs face unique challenges compared to terrestrial networks. The sensor nodes are battery-powered and operate in harsh environments where replacement is often impossible. The primary constraints include severe signal attenuation, limited bandwidth, and high propagation delays inherent to acoustic communication. Additionally, sparse deployment and dynamic 3D mobility can lead to connectivity issues and "coverage blind spots," making efficient routing and coverage strategies essential for network longevity [9].
P212|Normal|2.2 Clustering in WSN/UWSN
P213|Normal|Clustering is identified as one of the most suitable strategies for achieving energy stability and extending the lifetime of wireless sensor networks. In a clustered architecture, sensor nodes are organized into groups, with a specific node selected as the Cluster Head (CH). The CH is responsible for collecting, aggregating, and transmitting data to the Base Station (BS), reducing the transmission load on individual member nodes.
P214|Normal|Despite its benefits, the CH selection process presents significant challenges. CHs consume maximal energy due to the additional overload of data reception and aggregation. If CHs are selected randomly or without considering critical factors like residual energy, nodes may drain completely, leading to network deterioration. Therefore, optimizing CH selection is pivotal for energy conservation and avoiding the "hotspot problem" where nodes near the sink die prematurely.
P216|Normal|2.3 Meta-Heuristic Algorithms Used in Routing
P217|Normal|2.3.1 Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) & Improved GWO (IGWO)
P218|Normal|The Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) mimics the social hierarchy and hunting mechanism of grey wolves. The population is divided into four levels: Alpha (ɑ) (the leader), Beta (β) (deputy leader), Delta (δ) (subordinates), and Omega (ω) (lowest level). The optimization process involves tracking, encircling, and attacking prey, where the "prey" represents the optimal solution.
P219|Normal|The Improved Grey Wolf Optimizer (IGWO) introduces enhancements to address the standard GWO's limitations, such as premature convergence [3].
P220|Normal|Best Point Set Initialization: Instead of random initialization, IGWO uses a best point set method to ensure a more uniform distribution of the initial population, improving traversal performance.
P221|Normal|Non-linear Convergence Factor: The standard convergence parameter ɑ decreases linearly. IGWO adopts a non-linear adjustment strategy to better balance global exploration and local exploitation.
P222|Normal|Adaptive Weights: It utilizes dynamic leadership factors (w1, w2, w3) derived from the fitness values of the alpha, beta, and delta wolves to update positions more effectively.
P224|Normal|2.3.2 Golden Eagle Optimizer (GEO)
P225|Normal|The Golden Eagle Optimization Algorithm (GEOA) is a nature-inspired metaheuristic that mimics the spiral motion of golden eagles during hunting. The algorithm is defined by two distinct behaviors:
P226|Normal|Attack (Exploitation): The eagle moves towards the best-known prey location. This vector guides the population towards the best-visited locations.
P227|Normal|Cruise (Exploration): The eagle searches for better prey in the surrounding area. The cruise vector is calculated as a tangent to the circle perpendicular to the attack vector, allowing for linear movement relative to the prey.
P228|Normal|The strength of GEO lies in its ability to balance these phases using attack (PAttack) and cruise (PCruise) coefficients, which transition linearly over iterations. In the GEIGOA protocol, GEO is specifically utilized for the optimal selection of Cluster Heads by optimizing node centrality and residual energy[11].
P230|Normal|2.3.3 Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA) & Enhanced Versions
P231|Normal|The Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA) simulates the swarming behavior of grasshoppers, modeling both their repulsive forces (exploration/social distancing) and attractive forces (exploitation/food source attraction).
P232|Normal|In advanced routing protocols like GEIGOA, an Improved Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (IGHOA) is employed for path optimization and sink mobility. It determines the optimal route between the Cluster Head and the Base Station by assessing node degree, residual energy, and distance. The improved version often incorporates mechanisms to overcome local optima, ensuring reliable data transmission paths[11].

P318|Normal|3.1 Overview of the Proposed GEI–IGWO Hybrid Algorithm
P319|Normal|This research introduces a novel hybrid routing protocol designated as GEI-IGWO (Golden Eagle and Improved Grey Wolf Optimization). This protocol is specifically architected to withstand the harsh constraints of Underwater Wireless Sensor Networks (UWSNs), addressing the specific limitations of energy imbalance, security vulnerabilities, and high propagation latency.
P320|Normal|Why Hybridization?
P321|Normal|The "No Free Lunch" theorem posits that no single meta-heuristic algorithm is universally superior for all optimization problems. Hybridization is employed here to combine the complementary strengths of two distinct nature-inspired algorithms:
P322|Normal|Golden Eagle Optimization (GEO) for Exploration: GEO mimics the spiral cruising behavior of eagles. It is highly effective at global exploration, scanning the broad 3D underwater space to identify potential high-quality regions for Cluster Heads (CHs) without getting trapped in local optima.
P323|Normal|Improved Grey Wolf Optimization (IGWO) for Exploitation: IGWO excels at local exploitation through its hierarchical hunting mechanism (Alpha, Beta, Delta). Once GEO identifies a promising region, IGWO refines the search to pinpoint the exact best node with rapid convergence speed.
P324|Normal|GEIGOA Influence: The proposed model adopts the architectural success of the GEIGOA protocol (which hybridizes GEO with Grasshopper Optimization). However, it replaces the Grasshopper component with IGWO to enhance the precision of the local search and utilizes a new set of trust and QoS-based fitness parameters.
P326|Normal|3.2 Implementation Methodology
P327|Normal|3.2.1 Implementation Methodology - The UWSN is formed by randomly deploying underwater sensor nodes. A surface base station collects data. Each node is assigned parameters such as energy, position, and communication range to enable coordination.
P328|Normal|3.2.2 Optimised Cluster Head Selection - An optimization algorithm selects the best CHs using a fitness function that considers residual energy, node distance, and link quality. This ensures balanced load, reduced overhead, and prolonged network lifetime.
P329|Normal|3.2.3 Secure Data Transmission Phase - Cluster members send data to their CHs, which forward it to the base station via multi-hop routing. Lightweight encryption and authentication safeguard data integrity and prevent malicious access.
P330|Normal|3.2.4 Performance Evaluation and Real-Time Implementation- Simulations measure stability period, throughput, and network lifetime. A prototype validates feasibility under realistic underwater conditions.

P334|Normal|3.3 Working Flow of the Hybrid Algorithm
P335|Normal|The GEI-IGWO protocol operates in four distinct phases, ensuring a structured approach from network setup to active data routing.
P337|Normal|3.3.1 Phase 1: Initialization
P338|Normal|This phase sets up the network environment and prepares the optimization agents before the first round of data transmission begins.
P339|Normal|Network Deployment:
P340|Normal|N sensor nodes are randomly deployed in a 3D underwater monitoring area (A).
P341|Normal|Each node i is assigned fixed coordinates (xi, yi, zi) and initialized with a maximum energy level (Einit), sensing radius (Rs), and communication radius (Rc).
P342|Normal|The Base Station (Sink) is positioned at the water surface or a specific coordinate (e.g., 50, 100).
P343|Normal|Trust Module Initialization:
P344|Normal|A local trust table is created for every sensor node.
P345|Normal|Baseline values for Direct Trust (DT) and Indirect Trust (IT) are set to neutral (e.g. 0.5) to allow the Global Node Trust mechanism to evolve based on subsequent communication success or failure.
P346|Normal|Population Initialization (Best Point Set Strategy):
P347|Normal|Unlike traditional random initialization, which can lead to agents clumping in one area, this phase utilizes the Best Point Set method derived from the IGWO framework.
P348|Normal|This generates a population of search agents (representing candidate CHs) that are uniformly distributed across the search space, ensuring coverage of the entire monitoring area from the start.
P350|Normal|3.3.2 Phase 2: Cluster Head Selection using GEIGOA Logic (Exploration)
P351|Normal|The primary goal of this phase is Global Exploration. The algorithm scans the vast underwater environment to identify "promising regions" where high-quality Cluster Heads might be located. It uses the search logic of the Golden Eagle Optimizer (GEO) to avoid getting trapped in local optima (e.g., a node that is close but has low trust).
P352|Normal|Fitness Evaluation:
P353|Normal|Every search agent (Eagle) evaluates the quality of its current position (candidate node) using the 5 Parameter Fitness Function (Fmin). This function combines:
P354|Normal|Trust (f1): Is the node secure?
P355|Normal|Residual Energy (f2): Does it have enough battery?
P356|Normal|Distance to Sink (f3): Is it close to the surface?
P357|Normal|Node Density (f4): Is it central to other nodes?
P358|Normal|Path Delay (f5): Will data transmission be fast?
P359|Normal|Vector Calculation (Attack vs. Cruise):
P360|Normal|The movement of the eagles is dictated by two vectors:
P361|List Paragraph|Attack Vector (A): Moves the agent directly towards the best location found so far in its memory. This represents exploitation.
P362|List Paragraph|Cruise Vector (C): Moves the agent tangentially in a spiral motion relative to the prey. This represents exploration and allows the agent to search the neighborhood effectively.
P363|Normal|Position Update: The new position of an agent in the next iteration is calculated by summing these vectors, weighted by random coefficients. This allows the algorithm to aggressively pursue high-fitness nodes while still "cruising" to check if a better node exists nearby.
P365|Normal|3.3.3 Phase 3: IGWO-Based Refinement (Exploitation)
P366|Normal|Once Phase 2 identifies promising regions, Phase 3 aims for Local Exploitation. The Improved Grey Wolf Optimizer (IGWO) logic is triggered to refine the selection and lock onto the absolute best node within those identified regions.
P367|Normal|Leadership Hierarchy:
P368|Normal|The top three solutions found by the GEO phase are mapped to the IGWO hierarchy:
P369|Normal|Alpha (α): The best candidate CH found.
P370|Normal|Beta (β): The second-best candidate.
P371|Normal|Delta (δ): The third-best candidate.
P372|Normal|Omega (ω): All other search agents.
P373|Normal|Non-Linear Convergence Factor (a): Standard algorithms decrease their search radius linearly. IGWO uses a non-linear convergence factor (a) that decreases slowly at first and then rapidly. This allows for fine-tuning the search around the Alpha node to ensure precise selection.
P374|Normal|Protected Adaptive Mutation: During the exploitation phase (the final 40% of iterations), a protected mutation step is introduced. The algorithm applies an adaptive t-distribution mutation (trnd) to the Alpha node's position. This allows the best candidate to probabilistically test adjacent coordinates, ensuring the algorithm does not stagnate in local energy optima before finalizing the Cluster Head.
P375|Normal|Position Update with Adaptive Weights: The Omega agents update their positions based on the position of the Alpha, Beta, and Delta. Unlike standard GWO, IGWO uses dynamic weights (w1, w2, w3) calculated from the fitness of the leaders.

P378|Normal|3.3.4 Phase 4: Routing Path Optimization
P379|Normal|After CHs are selected, member nodes join clusters, and the network must establish routes to transmit data to the Sink. This phase uses logic from the Enhanced Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA) to handle routing and sink mobility.
P380|Normal|Sink Mobility: The Sink node is not static; it moves towards regions of the network that have high data density or low residual energy. This reduces the transmission distance for "weak" CHs, prolonging their life.
P381|Normal|Path Selection: Routes from CHs to the Sink are evaluated. A valid route must meet Quality of Service (QoS) standards. The fitness of a path is determined heavily by:
P382|Normal|Path Delay (f5): The sum of delays on all links must be minimized for real-time performance.
P383|Normal|Trust (f1): The route must not contain nodes flagged with low trust values in Phase 1.
P384|Normal|Data Transmission: Once the path is optimized, data is aggregated at the CH and transmitted along the chosen path to the mobile Sink. The energy consumed is deducted from the nodes, and the Trust Tables are updated based on the success or failure of the transmission, preparing the system for the next round (looping back to Phase 2).

CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
P478|Normal|6.1 Conclusion
P479|Normal|This research proposed a novel Hybrid GEI-IGWO (Golden Eagle and Improved Grey Wolf Optimization) routing protocol designed to address the multifaceted challenges of Underwater Wireless Sensor Networks (UWSNs). By hybridizing the global exploration capability of the Golden Eagle Optimizer with the precise local exploitation of the Improved Grey Wolf Optimizer, the proposed model successfully overcomes the limitations of single-objective algorithms, such as premature convergence and the "hotspot" problem .
P480|Normal|The core innovation of this study lies in the integration of a comprehensive 5-Parameter Fitness Function. Unlike traditional protocols that focus primarily on energy or distance, this model optimizes for Global Node Trust (security), Residual Energy (lifetime), Distance to Sink (energy efficiency), Intra-Cluster Compactness (stability), and Path Delay (QoS) simultaneously .
P481|Normal|Simulation results confirm that the GEI-IGWO protocol significantly outperforms benchmark methods (LEACH, IGWO, and GEIGOA). Specifically, it demonstrated:
P482|Normal|Extended Network Lifetime: By balancing energy consumption and preventing the selection of weak Cluster Heads, the stability period was extended by approximately 15-20% compared to competitive schemes .
P483|Normal|Enhanced Security: The trust model effectively filtered out unreliable nodes, leading to a higher packet delivery ratio and throughput .
P484|Normal|Superior QoS: The explicit minimization of path delay ensured that the protocol is suitable for time-sensitive underwater applications .
P486|Normal|6.2 Limitations
P487|Normal|Despite the promising results, this study has certain limitations:
P488|Normal|Computational Overhead: The hybrid nature of the algorithm involves calculating attack vectors, cruise vectors, and hierarchical leadership updates (Alpha, Beta, Delta) in every round. This introduces higher computational complexity compared to simple protocols like LEACH, which may strain the limited processing power of low-cost underwater sensor nodes.
P489|Normal|Idealized Channel Model: The simulations assumed ideal wireless channels or simplified propagation models (free space/multipath). It does not fully account for complex underwater acoustic phenomena such as severe Doppler shifts, thermocline effects, and temporary path loss due to marine life or turbulence.
P491|Normal|6.3 Future Work
P492|Normal|To further enhance the applicability of this protocol in real-world scenarios, future research will focus on:
P493|Normal|Reinforcement Learning Integration: Incorporating Reinforcement Learning (RL) could allow nodes to "learn" the best routing parameters dynamically over time, adapting to changing ocean conditions without re-running the full optimization logic every round.
P494|Normal|Mobility-Aware Sink Strategies: While this study implemented basic sink mobility, future work could explore intelligent, autonomous sink movement patterns (e.g., using AUVs) that predictively move towards energy-critical regions to harvest data.
P495|Normal|Realistic Acoustic Modeling: Future simulations will integrate realistic underwater acoustic propagation models to test the algorithm's robustness against high bit error rates and variable propagation speeds found in actual ocean environments.
P496|Normal|Heterogeneous Environments: Testing the algorithm in heterogeneous networks where nodes have different initial energy levels and sensing capabilities to better mimic real deployment scenarios.
